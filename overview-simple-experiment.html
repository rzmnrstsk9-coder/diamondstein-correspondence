This Project, Explained

This is a hundred-year-old family letter archive — 141 letters and postcards, most of them in Yiddish — being read, translated, and published in full for the first time, with AI doing the heavy lifting on the transcription and translation.

No one left in the family reads Yiddish well enough to check that work alone, so this project is also a real test of whether AI can do that kind of research honestly: every claim traces back to the actual scanned page, uncertain readings are marked as uncertain instead of smoothed into confident prose, and nothing gets invented to fill a gap.

That verified research doesn't just sit as raw data, either — it's been pulled together into an actual narrative, a real story about Sam and Dora with a beginning, middle, and end. But the story is assembled strictly from what the research supports: every scene traces back to a specific letter or fact, and where the record runs out, the story says so instead of quietly filling the gap.

The result is this site — the letters themselves, transcribed and translated, alongside the family story built from them, from research to narrative, with nothing invented along the way.
